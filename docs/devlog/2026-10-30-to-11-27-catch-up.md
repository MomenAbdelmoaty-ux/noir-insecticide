## What I set out to do

Finish Milestone A, the documents: turn my design notes into clean public requirements, a rulebook, decision records, and an architecture document, so the code can follow them. I was also learning how a professional project is organized while I did it.

## Timeline

- **October 30:** settled the open design questions: fixed health of 7 with recovery by kills, graded clipping, momentum kept after a shift with no coyote time, a class color for everything the player owns, stuck bugs that correct themselves, and hand-made art.
- **November 4 and 5:** added the final boss, chests and rarity by level, menus that freeze the game, weighted platforms and breakable blocks, bug movement rules, the horde cap, and a two-dimensional game and not 2.5D. Chose hand-made sections joined into levels, with spawn slots.
- **November 6 to 24:** refined the requirements and the rulebook through many rounds of questions and edits, and worked through the open design decisions, with the sections and spawn slots taking the longest. This took far longer than planned.
- **November 25:** merged the software requirements and the rulebook.
- **November 26:** merged the decision records and updates to ten older ones. Added the percentage damage rule for bugs and the chest screen.
- **November 27:** reworded the reachability guarantee, wrote the architecture document, the glossary, and the documentation index, and settled the multiplayer plan.

## Decisions made

- **Levels:** hand-made sections with one entrance and one exit, joined by hand for version 0.2.0 and assembled at random after it. Spawn slots decide what may appear, and how likely it is, but never where.
- **Chests:** a chest opens a screen with three weapons. The player picks one, and it drops in front of the chest. The other two are lost. The game freezes on that screen, except in multiplayer.
- **Damage to bugs:** traps and crushing deal a percentage of a bug's maximum health, set in data. The player still takes exactly 1. Elites and bosses are not affected.
- **Multiplayer:** keep the planned Node server. A peer-to-peer version over WebRTC is possible later, but a Node server is a stronger skill to show.
- **Two dimensions:** the game stays two-dimensional, with no depth.

## Learned

**Design and architecture**
- The strategy pattern and the factory pattern, and how a registry combines them.
- What the architecture of a game is: the simulation, the client, and how they talk.
- Data objects and state objects, and why game rules should not be wired to drawing, sound, or telemetry.
- What telemetry is: play statistics recorded automatically, such as where players die.
- The event bus, and why the display, sound, telemetry, and a future network connection all listen to events instead of being part of the rules.
- Object pooling, and measuring before pooling.

**Networking**
- Peer to peer, WebRTC, and signaling, and why a browser cannot act as a server.
- WebSockets versus Colyseus.
- What CORS is.

**Physics**
- Planck category bits and mask bits, and why two objects collide only when each one's mask includes the other's category.
- The defaults, and the group index.

**Levels and art**
- Spawn slots.
- Autotiling, terrain sets, and Wang sets.
- Texture atlases, texture baking, and pixels per game unit.
- Coyote time.

**Documents and tests**
